# Clout City: NYC — Cultural Authenticity Guide

Oct 20, 2026 · @Internovatechnologies

## Overview

This guide sorts a native New Yorker's notes into categories, scores each item, and maps the winners onto Clout City's mechanics. Priorities assume the current build: players start in Astoria, Flushing hosts the lottery, and other boroughs are locked. Queens items therefore outrank Manhattan icons for now.

Some notes were likely misheard. This guide uses the standard terms below.

| As noted | Standard term | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two Spices And A Coke | Two slices and a Coke | The classic pizza lunch combo |
| Dombo | DUMBO | Brooklyn neighborhood (Down Under the Manhattan Bridge Overpass) |
| Snog Harbour | Snug Harbor | Cultural center in Staten Island |
| Keweens | Kew Gardens (probably) | Queens neighborhood |
| Potts | Putz | Yiddish for idiot |
| A long walk | Schlep | Yiddish for a tiresome trek |
| Que le que | ¿Qué lo que? | Dominican Spanish for "what's up" |
| Navy week | Fleet Week | Late-May week when sailors visit |

"New York Manhattan" under the social calendar refers to the New Year's Eve countdown in Manhattan, covered under Social calendar.

## Ranking scale

Each item is scored 1–3 on four dimensions. **Score = A + D + F − E**, giving a range of 0 to 8.

| Dimension | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Authenticity (A) | Would a native New Yorker instantly say "yep, that's real"? |
| Delight (D) | How fun or surprising is it to discover in play? |
| Fit (F) | How well does it fit what's live now (Queens, Astoria, current mechanics)? |
| Effort (E) | Build cost: 1 = content only, 3 = new systems or 3D work |

| Score | Tier | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7–8 | Day 1 | Ship with the Astoria launch |
| 5–6 | Next wave | Within the next couple of versions |
| 3–4 | Later | Tied to borough unlocks or seasonal moments |
| 0–2 | Parked | Nice-to-have or flavor text only |

Two flags sit on top of the score. **IP** marks trademark risk under the no-real-brands rule. **Care** marks items touching identity or policing that could slide into stereotype.

## Neighborhoods and boroughs

Boroughs follow the unlock roadmap rather than a score: Queens now, then Manhattan, Brooklyn, the Bronx, and Staten Island.

| Unlock | Borough | Texture from the notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Now | Queens | Astoria, Flushing, Jamaica, Sunnyside, Kew Gardens, JFK and LaGuardia |
| 2nd | Manhattan | Harlem, SoHo, Chelsea, Tribeca, Lower East Side, Upper West Side (prestige), Hell's Kitchen, Financial District; gate behind clout as the aspirational borough |
| 3rd | Brooklyn | Williamsburg, Bushwick, Bed-Stuy, Crown Heights, Brownsville, DUMBO, LIU; creative economy vs. gentrification tension |
| 4th | Bronx | Yankee Stadium, Fordham, Belmont (the real Little Italy), Castle Hill, Longwood |
| 5th | Staten Island | St. George, Snug Harbor, Tompkinsville; older, conservative, "forgotten borough" |

**Care:** satirize Staten Island's attitude ("we're basically New Jersey"), not ethnic mob tropes.

**Gap to fill for launch:** Astoria is historically Greek (tavernas, cafés, the Greek Independence Day parade). Steinway Street is "Little Egypt," with Brazilian and Colombian pockets nearby. Flushing is a major Chinese and Korean hub known for its food courts. These matter more right now than anything in Manhattan.

## Civic life and communities

Communities and cultural invites score 6 but need the most care; policing stays ambient, and crime is not a mechanic.

| Item | Why it's NYC | A/D/F/E | Score | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Cultural event invites | Being invited to a Bar or Bat Mitzvah, quinceañera, or Greek baptism is a classic NYC experience | 3/3/2/2 | 6 | Care |
| NYPD presence | Visible everywhere; part of the city's texture | 3/1/2/2 | 4 | Care |
| Violent crime way down | Framing note: don't build crime as a mechanic or portray the city as dangerous | — | Framing |  |

Represent communities through food, festivals, language, and named NPCs with real personalities, never as punchlines. A Bar Mitzvah is for boys and a Bat Mitzvah is for girls; include both, with any player attending as a guest. Keep police ambient (officers at events, News satire) rather than interactive.

## Top 10 for day 1

These ten ship with the Astoria launch; all score 7–8 or fill the Queens gap.

- [ ] **The bodega and its cat.** A counter guy NPC remembers your order after three visits. The cat is pettable for a small Mood boost. Bacon egg and cheese is the cheapest breakfast in the game.
- [ ] **Dollar slice with a "two slices and a Coke" combo.** The budget Energy tier made iconic. A satirical News item can run when the price "inflates."
- [ ] **Halal cart open after midnight.** The only food late on the city clock, giving it a real gameplay reason to exist.
- [ ] **NPCs who talk like New Yorkers.** Slang in every line; "Deadass, it's brick out" on cold days.
- [ ] **A roommate at the start.** A roommate NPC splits the weekly rent. Moving out solo becomes the first big goal, fitting the $6,000 starting budget.
- [ ] **Dog walker as a first job.** An entry shift paying C$. Later, hiring your own dog walker is a status purchase.
- [ ] **Astoria Park.** Running as a daily task, picnics, and sitting to restore Mood. Summer concerts become World State events.
- [ ] **The subway as a character.** Astoria–Flushing travel by train, with fake announcements, delays, and showtime dancers giving a tiny clout bump.
- [ ] **Parody Mets home games in Flushing.** Attend as an event, buy a jersey, hear NPCs talk Queens vs. Bronx trash.
- [ ] **A cultural invite on your Phone.** For example, a Greek neighbor's name-day party. Dressing right earns clout; arriving empty-handed costs it.

## First-time experience

A new player should feel New York within 60 seconds of finishing the character creator, and Lola Lowdown, "Queens' most reliable unreliable source," is the voice that delivers it. Every first-session screen carries at least one real NYC detail, and Lola uses one or two slang terms per message, never more. Builds across C1–C3.

### First-session picks

| First | Pick | Why it lands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily tasks | Get a BEC from the bodega · Pet the bodega cat · Take a lap in Astoria Park | Three cheap, iconic actions that teach the Map, Energy, and Mood, plus the all-complete bonus |
| Job | Dog walker shift | Instantly recognizable NYC hustle; later flips into a status buy when you hire your own |
| Long-term goal | Get your own place (move out from the roommate) | Every New Yorker knows this goal; it gives weekly rent a purpose |
| Second goal | Become a regular (the bodega guy remembers your order after three visits) | Rewards coming back on day 2 and 3, supporting the 35–40% retention target |
| Places highlighted on the Map | Bodega, dollar slice, halal cart, Astoria Park, the N/W subway station | Cheap, open at different hours, and the core of the Day 1 list |
| Shop items featured | Weekly transit pass, puffer jacket, parody Mets cap, bodega cat plushie, the "I ♥ NY" shirt (Lola roasts anyone who buys it) | Affordable on the starting budget and each one carries a local joke |

### Lola's voice rules

- Gossipy, warm, a little dramatic; she's always "heard something."
- Calls the player "baby" or "sweetie," never by a slur or a cultural label.
- One or two slang terms per message, drawn from the slang table.
- Short: three brief sentences max in onboarding, so players read every line.
- Teases the next thing (a shift, a goal, a piece of tea) so the session always has a pull forward.
